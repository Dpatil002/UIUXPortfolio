01 / 02 / 03 · THE CORE ECOSYSTEM

One Platform, Three Audiences

01 · Platform Overview

Compassionate Care with Reliable Operations

Elixir Helps connects individuals facing anxiety, burnout, depression, or marital hurdles with vetted, empathetic counsellors for confidential online video sessions across India. It supports individual therapy, couple's therapy, child counselling, stress management, and corporate workshops.

A mental health product carries extraordinary emotional stakes: it must build immediate safety for someone in distress, fiercely protect client privacy, and simultaneously equip the business and clinicians to coordinate hundreds of therapy hours smoothly.

02 · Problem Statement

Hesitation on the Front-End, Friction on the Back-End

The Client Hesitation: People who need therapy frequently postpone booking because the first step feels intimidating: “Who is this counsellor? Is it private? How does online therapy even work? What if it's awkward?”

The Operational Chaos: Behind the scenes, a scaling clinic requires an all-in-one hub to coordinate schedules, enforce two-factor verification, prevent therapist no-shows, process UPI payments, and maintain strict data privacy compliance.

03 · Project Goals

What Success Looked Like

TRUST

Make the first step feel safe

Warm, human visuals, counsellor video introductions, and transparent fees so booking feels personal, never clinical.

FINDABILITY

Help people find the right fit

Condition pages that explain feelings first in plain words, followed by matching accredited counsellors.

CONVERSION

Book in a few steps

Select a counsellor, pick an open calendar slot, verify duration & fee, and complete confirmation without surprise roadblocks.

PRIVACY

Protect client identity

Clients appear strictly as Anonymized IDs in internal tools, admin access is recorded to audit logs, and logins enforce 2FA.

OPERATIONS

Run business in one place

Real-time status overview of today's consults, revenue splits, counsellor onboarding, and no-show alerts.

COUNSELLOR FOCUS

Less admin, more therapy

A portal answering "what's next?" instantly between consultations, with one-tap Teams meeting launch and simple notes.

11 / 12 / 13 / 14 / 15 / 16 · INTERNAL TOOLS

Calm, Dense, and Private by Default

11.1 · Super Admin

Operations & Compliance Lead

“I need to know in 10 seconds if today is on track, and who to call if it isn't.”

Core Jobs: Onboard and suspend therapists, monitor sessions and no-shows, reconcile payments, track earnings, conduct workshops, export audit logs.

Design Priority: Maximum information density, exceptions surfaced as priority alerts, strict role-based access.
11.2 · Counsellor Portal

Therapist Running 5–7 Sessions a Day

“Between sessions I have five minutes. Just tell me who's next and let me join.”

Core Jobs: Review daily schedule, launch video consultation, mark attendance, record notes, check client rating (4.8★), view payout history.

Design Priority: Zero cognitive fatigue, immediate "now and next" focus, one prominent launch button, no unnecessary data clutter.
14 · Admin V1 Iteration

A Friendly First Version, and Why It Changed

V1 carried the website's soft illustration aesthetic into the back-office: a "Good Morning, Jake" illustration greeting, big KPI cards, patient avatars, and rich personal profiles. While visually charming, it failed real administrative work and violated privacy mandates:

V1 Approach The Critical Problem Final Design Solution
Illustrated hero greeting Occupied 33% of the viewport without providing actionable intelligence. Compact "Today's Overview" header with date, starting immediate operational data at pixel 0.
Patient names on cards Exposed confidential patient identity to anyone passing an admin screen. Strict Anonymized Client IDs (e.g. CL001, CLIENT-1847) enforced across all internal tooling.
Full personal profile Birthday, personal address, and private clinical notes exposed to non-clinical staff. Need-to-know permissions: therapy notes isolated to clinician portal; every admin view logged to Audit Trail.
Card grid for lists Displayed only six records per viewport. Painful to scan, filter, or bulk-manage. Dense, scannable data tables with status pills, instant keyboard search, and deep column filtering.
4 vanity KPI counters Cumulative historical totals told management nothing about issues requiring intervention today. Operational KPIs (Today's, Ongoing, Missed Sessions) plus an automated high/medium priority Alerts module.
Standard single-factor login Inadequate security for a database containing sensitive health-adjacent records. Enforced Two-Factor Authentication (2FA) and granular role-based permissions in Settings.
15 · Super Admin (13 Screens)

Key Functional Modules

  • Today's Overview: Live answers to "Is today running smoothly?" with colour-coded timeline (Completed, Live, Upcoming).
  • Client Management: Anonymized IDs only. Missed consults highlighted in red, with payment status and issue flags.
  • Alerts & Monitoring: Repeated no-shows, schedule overlaps, and session over-runs triaged High, Medium, or Low.
  • Audit Logs: Every admin view or export of a therapist or session logs timestamp, admin username, and IP address.
16 · Counsellor Portal (9 Screens)

Built for Clinical Flow

  • "Session Starting Soon" Banner: Prominent banner appearing 10 minutes prior with one-click "Join Teams Meeting".
  • Today's Timeline: Scannable daily agenda showing next patient and sessions completed so far.
  • Client History & Feedback: Access to assigned Client IDs, past session dates, and 4.8★ patient feedback score.
  • Earnings & Payouts: Transparent monthly totals, completed sessions, and automated payout disbursement schedule.
17 · SYSTEMATIC PSYCHOLOGY

The Principles Behind Both Products

How foundational UX laws were applied differently across an emotional client-facing website versus a data-heavy administrative suite:

UX Law Applied on Public Website Applied on Admin & Counsellor Portals
01 · Jakob's Law Familiar appointment pill booking calendar that mirrors patterns users already trust. Standard SaaS pattern: fixed left sidebar navigation, clear page subtitles, and filterable tables.
02 · Hick's Law One primary CTA per view ("Book Appointment", "Talk to Kavya"). Counsellor dashboard displays one bold action ("Join Teams Meeting") only on the upcoming session.
03 · Miller's Law Therapist specializations grouped into small chip clusters; homepage spotlights 4 therapists. Operational dashboard constrained to 4 primary KPIs; exceptions prioritized High, Med, Low.
04 · Chunking 13–16 section long-form service pages broken into visual cards, illustrated steps, and tables. Sidebar partitioned by job category: Run Today, People, Financials, Insights, Compliance.
05 · Fitts's Law Full-width "Talk to Kavya" CTA directly beneath fee and duration; 44px+ mobile touch targets. Generous table row hit areas and full-width "Join" button situated directly where clinician eye rests.
06 · Von Restorff Effect One distinctive italic purple serif word per heading anchors emotional reassurance. Missed sessions highlighted in red; flagged patients marked with warning icons; upcoming sessions in amber.
07 · Law of Proximity Fee, session duration, and booking CTA grouped in one "Session Details" container beside calendar. Therapist name, Client ID, and status pill neatly stacked within identical row container.
08 · Goal-Gradient "Your path to mental wellness" in 3 clear milestones: choose expert → schedule → start consultation. "Completed today: 2" counter and lifetime consult milestones give clinicians tangible daily momentum.
09 · Tesler's Law Time zone calculation and therapist availability handled automatically; user simply selects a date. Revenue splits, payout calculations, and PDF invoices synthesized automatically with 1-click export.
10 · Aesthetic-Usability Calm mint and lavender backgrounds, authentic photos, and delicate illustrations soften anxiety. Balanced white space and tabular figures make high data density comfortable during 8-hour shifts.
11 · Serial Position Pages begin with empathy ("You're not alone") and conclude with "Ready to begin your healing?". Dashboards begin with today's immediate queue and conclude with platform announcements.
12 · Doherty Threshold Lightweight booking widget renders instant time slot availability upon tapping calendar date. Filter-first tables and client-side pagination maintain sub-400ms UI responsiveness for large tables.

19 · Sensitive Contexts

Trauma-Aware, Privacy-First

  • Feelings-First Microcopy: Speaks empathetically ("You're not alone", "Your feelings matter") and avoids high-pressure sales triggers.
  • Zero Client Names: Patients are never addressed by name in administrative tables; strictly Anonymized Client IDs.
  • Audit Logging: Every admin record view or export logs timestamp, username, and IP for regulatory peace of mind.
  • Crisis Protocols: Clinicians receive clear, immediate access to suicide and crisis-intervention workflows in their resource tab.
20 · Accessibility

Built Toward WCAG 2.1 AA

  • High Contrast Ratio: Deep teal #2B7A78 on white surfaces easily surpasses the 4.5:1 ratio for text and interactive controls.
  • Dual-Signal States: Clinical status is communicated with text labels plus color (never color alone).
  • 44px+ Hit Targets: Booking chips and navigation pills adhere to generous tap targets for mobile browsers.
  • Accessible Media: Video introductions feature visible playback controls and captions for hearing-impaired users.

22.1 · Core Learnings

What I Learned

On-brand isn't always on-task

The illustrated admin V1 looked lovely but slowed actual operators down. Internal tools require the brand's calm demeanor, not its visual decoration.

Privacy is a UX decision, not just a backend one

Shifting from patient names to Client IDs altered every single table, search index, card, and exception flow across both operational applications.

Design QA after handoff is essential

Side-by-side verification with engineers intercepted omitted sections and misapplied CSS values before end patients were affected.
