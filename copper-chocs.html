01 · Background & Heritage

From Handmade Chocolates to a City-Wide Name

Copper Chocs started in 1995 with handmade chocolates sold in Pune supermarkets. It grew into cakes, pastries, cookies, savouries, and bespoke celebration cakes, with 15 outlets across the city.

Customers knew the counters and trusted the taste in person. However, the online presence had to earn the exact same trust for orders placed in seconds from a phone or computer.

02 · The Core Problem

Cakes Are Bought With the Eyes, and in a Hurry

Celebration orders are emotional and time-boxed. Customers compare bakeries in seconds and drop off at the slightest doubt:

❓ Dietary certainty: "Is it 100% eggless? Will my family be able to eat it?"
✍️ Personalisation: "Can I add custom wording or a birthday name without calling?"
⏰ Delivery timing: "Will it arrive tomorrow before the party starts at 4 PM?"
📍 Local proximity: "Where is the nearest outlet if I want to pick it up fresh myself?"
CORE DESIGN GOAL

Eliminate every point of friction before the "Add to Cart" button so customers can configure a cake and complete their checkout in under 60 seconds.

01

Look as good as it tastes

Premium, consistent food photography system across every hero banner, category card, and product page.

02

Order in a few taps

"Order Now" accessible within a single scroll everywhere, reducing time from inspiration to cart.

03

Remove last-minute doubt

Eggless toggle, custom cake message, serving sizes, and delivery date picker answered directly inline.

04

Bring people into stores

A true first-class store finder for walk-ins, phone enquiries, and in-person pickups across all 15 Pune shops.

03 / 04 · BENCHMARKING & USER JOBS

Benchmarking India's Leading Bakery Brand

03 · Competitor Audit: Theobroma

Auditing What Customers Already Expect vs Gaps to Own

I audited Theobroma's online store (specifically their Brownies & Cakes collection) to understand what customers across India already expect from a modern bakery experience, and where a storied regional brand like Copper Chocs could win.

UX Pattern What I Saw (Theobroma) My Strategic Response (Copper Chocs)
Navigation Left filter by product type only. Filter rail by best sellers, product type, flavour, and price points.
Imagery One consistent photo style across the catalog grid. A defined, multi-category AI-assisted image system for every product and hero.
Card Info Price and CTA on every card. Price on cards; full decision purchase flow grouped on the dedicated product page.
Dietary Egg / eggless buried as a sidebar filter. Prominent egg toggle at point of purchase plus a clear "100% Eggless" trust badge.
Gap to Own No store finder or custom-cake journey. A 15-outlet dedicated locator and occasion-first customisation flow.

07 · PSYCHOLOGY & TRADEOFFS

Five Decisions That Shaped the Product

Each decision started from an explicit customer doubt, required a conscious design trade-off, and is directly grounded in cognitive psychology.

01

Products Before Promotions

USER DOUBT
“What do they actually sell here?”
The Decision: Replaced the seasonal promo banner strip with a photo-led carousel of six core categories placed immediately below the hero.
TRADE-OFF MADE Less real estate reserved for promotional marketing campaigns. Campaigns were migrated into the rotating hero carousel so they never push the product catalog below the fold.
🧠 Miller's Law · Recognition Over Recall
BEFORE · V1

Generic seasonal banner strip ("Taste the joy of the season") blocking immediate category scannability.

AFTER · FINAL

"Explore Our Delights" — 6 clear visual category cards with fresh pastry imagery immediately scannable.

02

Answer Every Doubt Before "Add to Cart"

USER DOUBT
“Is it eggless? Can I add a custom name? Will it arrive tomorrow?”
The Decision: Consolidated weight pills (½ kg / 1 kg), the 100% eggless toggle, cake message input, postal code delivery check, and same-day date picker into one cohesive purchase panel on the product page.
TRADE-OFF MADE A longer purchase panel. However, strict hierarchical spacing and grouped micro-cards keep it effortless to scan with a single, clear primary button.
🧠 Tesler's Law · Law of Proximity
Weight: ½ Kg or 1 Kg Eggless Switch
Personalization input: "Happy 5th Birthday Anaya!"
Add to Cart — ₹750
03

Customisation Starts from the Occasion

USER DOUBT
“I don't know what to ask for. I just know it's a wedding.”
The Decision: Created four prominent occasion shortcuts (Birthday, Corporate, Wedding, Anniversary) instead of forcing customers through an intimidating, empty custom-order form.
TRADE-OFF MADE Fine dietary and design details are captured progressively on the subsequent inquiry step rather than front-loading every requirement.
🧠 Hick's Law · Reduced Cognitive Overhead
🎂 Birthday
🏢 Corporate
💍 Wedding
🥂 Anniversary
04

The Store Finder Is a Product, Not a Footer Link

USER DOUBT
“Which shop is near me, and is it open right now?”
The Decision: Designed a dedicated, top-level store locator page with Pune locality search, live opening hours, tap-to-call, driving directions, and an "Order Now for Pickup" CTA.
TRADE-OFF MADE Adds another top-level page to the navigation hierarchy. Completely justified for a 30-year legacy brand where in-person pickup represents substantial revenue.
🧠 Fitts's Law & Jakob's Law
Copper Chocs — Baner, Pune
Open Mon–Sat 10:00 AM – 10:00 PM
Call Now Visit Now
05

One Image Language Across the Whole Store

USER DOUBT
“Is this an artisan bakery or just a local corner shop?”
The Decision: Formulated an uncompromising art direction guideline (warm directional side light, blush/linen backgrounds, texture close-ups, and floating chocolate shards for hero moments) and applied it across every category.
TRADE-OFF MADE Required significant up-front art direction and curation time. In return, every future product photography shoot now possesses a strict, repeatable brand brief.
🧠 Aesthetic-Usability Effect
Warm side lighting, linen ground, gold pastry cutlery.

13 / 14 / 15 · ART DIRECTION & SYSTEM

Art Direction at the Speed of a Launch

13 · AI in My Workflow

Art Direction Framework

The brand had no consistent, high-resolution photography library across every dessert and savoury category. Rather than holding back the launch, I developed a strict four-stage AI generation framework and composited the final creative assets in Figma:

1

Brief

Directional warm side light, blush or linen ground, antique gold cutlery, and pastry texture close-ups.

2

Generate

Dozens of high-resolution variations per category strictly following lighting and palette prompts.

3

Curate

Ruthlessly rejected any image that misrepresented the real handmade bakery texture, finish, or proportions.

4

Composite

Added Copper Chocs branding hearts, calibrated color tone, and added negative copy space in Figma.

Category System: Sweet Bakes (Hero) · Savouries · Tea-Time Snacks Curated & Composited in Figma

15 · Cognitive Psychology in Action

UX Principles Applied

UX Law / Principle How It Was Applied in the Interface
Hick's Law One clear primary CTA per hero slide; four occasion shortcuts rather than an overwhelming 12-field form.
Miller's Law Strict limit of six core categories; customer trust promises grouped into three scannable cards at a time.
Fitts's Law Full-width "Add to Cart" button placed directly beside the quantity stepper for thumb-friendly reachability.
Tesler's Law Delivery date picker, postal verification, and custom message integrated directly onto the product page rather than deferred to checkout.
Von Restorff Effect The "Most Loved Treats" featured spotlight intentionally breaks the repeating 3x2 grid rhythm to drive discovery.
Aesthetic-Usability Effect Unified, warm photography and cohesive typography immediately elevate perceived trust for phone orders.

17 · Critical Reflections

What I Learned

For food, photography is the UX

A consistent, mouthwatering image system did significantly more to establish perceived luxury and buyer confidence than any layout tweak could achieve alone.

Promotions expire, the range doesn't

Leading with clear product categories made the homepage useful all 365 days of the year, preventing post-holiday banner staleness.

Bakeries are truly omnichannel

Treating the 15-outlet store finder with the same prominence as the shopping cart respected how multi-decade loyal customers actually buy.
