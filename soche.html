01 — OVERVIEW

Pure. Potent. Premium.

Soché (a registered trademark of Le Papillon Boutique Ltd, UK) launched with Tellicherry black peppercorns and Pink Himalayan salt, with turmeric, star anise, chilli, cinnamon and more expanding the collection. Its single-origin spices are procured directly from heritage agricultural centres—including Kerala's pepper growers and Sikkim's cardamom hills—grounded in complete batch traceability, farmer fair pay, and Rainforest Alliance-aligned sourcing.

From day one, the digital platform had to perform two radically different business functions under a unified brand identity:

Job 01: Retail D2C

Convince a London home cook that a jar of pepper is worth £14.50

Replace supermarket commoditization with an intimate, sensory story of harvest, late-crop berry selection, sun-drying on the Malabar Coast, and high-piperine potency.

Job 02: B2B Wholesale

Convince a procurement manager that Soché can supply 5 tonnes reliably

Prove monthly tonnage capacity, export compliance (HACCP, ISO 22000, BRC Food, UK FSA), batch certificates of analysis, bulk packaging specs, and verifiable origin supply chains.

02 — THE CHALLENGE

Commodity vs. Craft: The Cultural Dilemma

"Spices were once treasured across civilisations—navigated across oceans and exchanged like gold. Today they sit on supermarket shelves as cheap commodities in plastic grinders. How do you make someone feel the difference before they've tasted it, and sell the same product to a home chef and an industrial wholesaler?"

The Client Brief: Le Papillon Boutique Ltd approached The Impact Engine to create the digital flagship for Soché's UK launch. The objectives were uncompromising: establish an undeniable luxury aesthetic, bridge the credibility gap for high-volume trade procurement, and build on Shopify with a zero-friction path from discovery to repeat purchase.

07 — RETAIL D2C EXPERIENCE

Slow Down, Look Closer, Then Buy

The Homepage: A Brand Film, Then the Product

Rather than assaulting the user with discount banners and popups, the homepage opens on an atmospheric brand film: "The Soché Story: a symphony of nature, craft & heart". It introduces the earth, hands, and origin mist. As the visitor scrolls, the two launch products appear in hand-held packaging shots, grounding the digital experience in tactile reality.

The narrative progresses deliberately:

  • The Calling: Why supermarket spices lost their soul and how Soché restores purity.
  • Direct Origins: Imagery and coordinates of Kerala's pepper vines and Himalayan crystalline beds.
  • "Beyond the Harvest": Fair pricing guarantees, batch traceability, and regenerative agricultural stewardship.
  • The Coming-Soon Shelf: Organic cardamom, turmeric, and star anise creating anticipation for future drops.
  • Closing Statement: "We don't merely trade in spices, we carry legacies forward."

A Product Page That Reads Like a Journey

Instead of an industrial specification sheet or generic Shopify product template, the Tellicherry Pepper page follows the life cycle of the berry. A hand-illustrated, S-curve dotted path guides the reader through five distinct chapters:

  1. The Malabar Coast: Micro-climate, soil chemistry, and tropical monsoon mist.
  2. The Harvest: Late-crop harvesting ensuring maximum berry maturity and piperine density.
  3. Sun-Drying: Traditional bamboo mats allowing slow, natural curing under the Indian sun.
  4. Hand Selection: Rigorous grading separating whole extra-bold peppercorns (TGSEB grade).
  5. Aroma-Sealed Packing: Airtight UV-protective glass jars keeping volatile essential oils intact until uncapped.

The Motion Prototype: I built this journey as a fully responsive coded prototype in HTML/CSS/JS (using Prata and Libre Baskerville typography, intersection observers, and scroll-triggered SVG reveals) so engineering could replicate the exact deceleration and motion pacing.

Shop / All Products: A Gallery Wall, Not a Supermarket Aisle

Products sit in a generous 3-column layout punctuated by full-bleed farm and soil photography, maintaining high aesthetic tension. Custom filter pills (All, Best Seller, Coming Soon) and facet dropdowns (Flavour, Usage, Category) ensure effortless browsing as the catalog expands to dozens of SKUs.

08 — WHOLESALE B2B PORTAL

Speaking a Procurement Buyer's Language

Commercial trade buyers do not purchase spices by the jar—they evaluate supply continuity, lab compliance, and customs documentation. Each round pushed further away from "shop" and closer to "commercial supply partner":

v1 · Retail in Disguise

Supermarket pattern

  • "Global spices trusted by experts" hero banner over generic spice jar photo.
  • Product grid with consumer star ratings and "Buy now" buttons.
  • Vague private-label text and generic inquiry box.
  • The Flaw: Procurement directors saw right through it. Nothing proved supply volume or regulatory credentials.
v2 · Export-Grade Content

Industrial Metrics

  • Modern warehouse hero with capacity KPIs (Metric Tonnes/month, verified partners).
  • 9 technical capability cards and compliance document archive.
  • Filterable SKU spec table with moisture and grade limits.
  • Bulk packaging options (25kg bags to multi-tonne totes).
Final · Sovereign B2B Identity

Deep Forest-Green Portal

  • Deep forest-green palette (#1A3B2A) providing immediate visual demarcation from consumer retail.
  • 8 tightened capability pillars and end-to-end supply chain visibility.
  • International compliance accreditation: HACCP, ISO 22000, BRC Food, UK FSA.
  • Commercial RFP portal with spec attachment upload and direct trade WhatsApp hotline.
Why a Distinct Color Register?

Deep forest green immediately signals "enterprise & trade" the instant a buyer transitions, while reinforcing the Rainforest Alliance agricultural sustainability connection. By keeping the typography, logo, and warm cream base intact, one brand communicates fluently with two disparate audiences without diluting either.

The initial wireframe version (v1) utilized standard corporate blocks: Origins → Philosophy → The Craft → Legacy → The Soché Ritual. While aesthetically clean, user feedback indicated it felt like an abstract corporate monologue rather than an invitation to experience luxury.

We overhauled the page entirely, rewriting it as an episodic 9-chapter narrative designed to read with the literary cadence of a published book:

Chapter 1
Before the Kitchen
Food starts long before the stovetop—in the ancient soils, microclimates, and generational farmers who cultivate it.
Chapter 2
A Question Worth Asking
Why did spices that once built trade empires and defined cultures become forgotten supermarket commodities?
Chapter 3
Returning to the Source
The search across India’s primary growing regions, sitting with smallholders to understand heirloom cultivars.
Chapter 4
Quality Over Convenience
The non-negotiable quality and compliance protocol: lab testing for moisture, piperine potency, and pesticide-free guarantee.
Chapter 5
Respecting Nature's Work
Certified by Nature: committing to Rainforest Alliance standards, protecting biodiverse ecosystems and fair living wages.
Chapter 6
The Journey Continues
Farm → testing → climate-controlled packing → kitchen table. Full provenance preserved at every mile.
Chapter 7
More Than a Spice Brand
Reviving the reverence for unadulterated flavor in modern European households.
Chapter 8
Looking Forward
A legacy of uncompromising flavor, winning culinary trust one pantry at a time across the UK.

Our Promise

10 — BEHAVIORAL PSYCHOLOGY

The Psychology Behind a Premium Experience

Every visual and architectural choice maps directly to validated behavioral heuristics from the Laws of UX:

01

Aesthetic-Usability Effect

Users perceive aesthetically pleasing designs as more usable and credible.
Editorial food styling, warm cream canvases, and balanced serifs justify a luxury price point before a single product claim is read.
02

Jakob's Law

Users spend most of their time on other sites and expect yours to work the same way.
Under the luxury editorial veneer, standard e-commerce patterns remain intact: top-right cart, recognizable filter pills, and instant Shopify checkout.
03

Hick's Law

Decision time increases logarithmically with the number and complexity of choices.
Streamlined primary navigation (Shop, Our Story, Wholesale, Contact) and a focused launch collection of just two hero products.
04

Miller's Law

The average person can hold 7 (± 2) items in immediate working memory.
The pepper provenance journey is 5 chapters, quality pillars are 6, and wholesale capabilities were reduced from 9 down to 8 cohesive units.
05

Chunking

Information broken into logical, manageable chunks is easier to scan and retain.
Our Story is chunked into 9 episodic chapters. Recipe ingredients are grouped by preparation step. Wholesale specs sit in a filterable matrix.
06

Goal-Gradient Effect

User motivation accelerates as they perceive themselves nearing completion.
Numbered journey steps (01–05) and a 4-step wholesale verification timeline show tangible progress from harvest to kitchen.
07

Von Restorff Effect

The item that differs visually from its peers is the one most readily remembered.
The deep forest-green Wholesale register breaks instantly from the maroon retail tone. Full-bleed soil images disrupt regular product card rhythms.
08

Law of Proximity

Elements placed close together are perceived as sharing common functionality.
On culinary recipe pages, the exact Soché spice used sits directly underneath the step-by-step method, accompanied by its own inline Buy Now trigger.
09

Tesler's Law (Complexity)

Every process has an inherent amount of complexity that cannot be eliminated, only moved.
Trade buyers submit one structured, single-pane form (product, volume, destination). The Soché ops team absorbs all backend logistics and customs coordination.
10

Peak-End Rule

People remember the start, emotional apex, and final touchpoints of an experience most vividly.
Pages open on an evocative brand film and close on a high-resonance founder quote: "True luxury is defined by integrity."
11

Fitts's Law

Target acquisition time depends on distance to and width of the target.
Sticky cart in header with 48px+ touch targets on mobile. Prominent "Shop Launch Collection" CTAs stationed at the conclusion of every narrative block.

12 — TRUST & ACCESSIBILITY

Every Claim Backed & WCAG 2.1 AA Rigor

Content & Verification Signals

  • Named Origins: Specificity replaces marketing fluff (Tellicherry, Malabar Coast; Idukki, Kerala; cardamom hills of Sikkim).
  • Rainforest Alliance Seal: Official badge embedded alongside interactive modal explaining smallholder audit protocols.
  • Visible Compliance Chain: Sourcing verified $\rightarrow$ Lab tested for aflatoxins and moisture $\rightarrow$ Batch confirmed $\rightarrow$ Final QC.
  • Real Human Faces: Verified farmers and founding directors featured in real photography instead of generic stock models.

Accessibility: Luxury Shouldn't Mean Hard to Read

  • Contrast Compliance: Deep maroon text on cream grounds yielding 7:1+ contrast (far surpassing WCAG AA 4.5:1). Light text only used over dark overlays.
  • Legible Photography Overlays: Dynamically darkened scrim gradients beneath text on full-bleed imagery to guarantee headline readability.
  • Reduced-Motion Respect: CSS prefers-reduced-motion: reduce triggers instant reveals, preserving 100% of information for vestibular sensitivity.
  • 48px Tap Targets: All buttons, accordion headers, and filter pills meet or exceed accessible mobile touch target guidelines.
  • Screen-Reader Friendly Recipes: Ingredients structured as semantic unordered lists and cooking methods as numbered <ol> steps.

v2 Optimization Roadmap (From Live Site UX Audit)

1. Show Prices Upfront on Collection Grids The initial launch omitted prices on category grids. Introducing "From £14.50" reduces pre-click user hesitation and qualifies clicks.
2. "Notify Me" Capture for Coming Soon SKUs Much of the botanical range (cardamom, turmeric, star anise) is coming soon. Inline email capture turns curiosity into immediate launch day pipeline.
3. Verified Buyer Reviews Integration Integrate verified culinary reviews with photo uploads on product pages as early cohort orders complete.
4. Homepage Redundancy Cleanup Consolidate duplicated peppercorn CTA sections on the homepage discovered during early heatmapping audits.
5. Standardize British English Spelling Eliminate instances where copy drifted between "Flavor" and "Flavour". Mandate UK English standards across all digital surfaces.
6. Surface Direct Contact Details in Footer Expose UK support email and trade WhatsApp directly in the site-wide footer rather than isolating them strictly to the Contact page.
